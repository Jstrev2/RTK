# Runner Toolkit — Writing Voice

Every Rundown guide, tool description, and email gets written in this voice.
The goal is simple: sound like a runner who knows what they're talking about,
writing to a training partner. Not a content farm. Not an AI.

## The voice in one paragraph

You've run long enough to have opinions and scars. You respect the reader's
time and intelligence. You'd rather recommend one shoe with a caveat than
list ten without one. You use real numbers because you actually looked them
up. When the honest answer is "it depends," you say what it depends on and
then commit to a default. You're never above the reader — you've made every
mistake you warn about.

## Hard rules

**Be specific or cut it.** "The Winflo 12 runs $110 and weighs 9.7 oz" earns
its sentence. "There are many great budget options available" does not.
Every guide should survive this test: could a competitor paste this
paragraph on their site? If yes, it's too generic — add the detail only we
bothered to check.

**One opinion per topic, tradeoff attached.** Recommend a default, name who
it's wrong for. Comprehensive lists are what we built the
[shoe finder](/tools/shoe-selector) for; prose is where we commit.

**Numbers come from the database or a named source.** Prices, weights,
drops, dates — check them against /shoes data before publishing. If research
is thin or contested, say so plainly: "one study, never replicated, so treat
it as a hint."

**Short paragraphs. Vary the rhythm.** Two to four sentences, then
occasionally one. If three sentences in a row have the same shape, rewrite
one.

**Headers are claims, not labels.** "Your second pair should be lighter, not
newer" beats "Choosing a Second Shoe." A reader skimming only headers should
absorb the argument.

**End when the argument ends.** The last line is a next step or a blunt
restatement of the one thing that matters — never a summary that begins
"Ultimately" or "In conclusion."

**Friendly, never accusatory.** We warn about mistakes as someone who has
made them ("I've bonked at mile 22 doing exactly this"), not as someone
scolding. No doom framing, no "you're doing it wrong."

## The banned list (AI tells and filler)

If any of these appear, the draft isn't done:

- "delve," "crucial," "essential," "game-changer," "elevate," "unleash,"
  "robust," "seamless," "in today's world," "the world of running"
- "It's important to note," "It's worth mentioning," "Keep in mind"
- "Whether you're a beginner or a seasoned marathoner" and every other
  whether-you're construction
- Rule-of-three adjective stacks ("light, responsive, and versatile")
- An intro that restates the title in more words
- A conclusion section
- Bullet lists where every bullet has identical grammatical structure and
  length (real lists are lumpy)
- Bold-keyword-colon-explanation formatting repeated more than twice
- Rhetorical questions as transitions ("So what does this mean for your
  training?")
- Hedging stacked on hedging ("may potentially help some runners")

## Mechanics

- Contractions, always. "You'll" not "you will."
- "You" is the reader. "We" is Runner Toolkit and appears only when we mean
  the site (our data, our tools, our disclosure).
- Internal links go in mid-sentence on descriptive anchors: "a
  [stability trainer like the GT-1000](/shoes/asics-gt-1000-15) holds up" —
  not "click here," not bare URLs, not a links section at the bottom.
  Three to six per guide, only where a reader would actually want them.
- Bodies use the renderer's subset only: paragraphs, `##` and `###`
  headings, `- ` lists, `[text](url)` links, `**bold**`. No tables, no
  numbered lists, no images without a real cover image.
- Titles: sentence-meaning over keyword-stuffing, but the head query should
  appear naturally ("Good Running Shoes Under $120" contains its query).
- Excerpts are two sentences max and concrete enough to stand alone in a
  search snippet.

## The read-aloud test

Before publishing, read the draft out loud. Anywhere you wouldn't say the
sentence to a runner at your kitchen table, rewrite it in the words you'd
actually use. Then cut 10% — it's always there.
