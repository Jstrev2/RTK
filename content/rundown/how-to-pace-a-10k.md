**Short answer:** Run mile 1 about 5 seconds slower than goal pace, hold goal pace to mile 5, then spend what's left. A 50:00 10K is 8:03 a mile, or 5:00 a kilometer.

## Goal pace comes from one division

A 10K is 6.2137 miles. Divide your goal time by that number to get the mile pace.

- 40:00 is 6:26 a mile, 4:00 a kilometer.
- 50:00 is 8:03 a mile, 5:00 a kilometer.
- 60:00 is 9:39 a mile, 6:00 a kilometer.
- 70:00 is 11:16 a mile, 7:00 a kilometer.

The [pace calculator](/tools/pace-calculator) prints a split card for any other goal, and the [pace guide for a 2-hour half](/rundown/pace-for-a-2-hour-half-marathon) shows the same method over a longer distance.

## Start 5 seconds slow, not fast

The first mile of a 10K is crowded and adrenaline makes pace feel easy. A fast first mile is the usual way a 10K goes wrong, because the effort stays high and the last miles slow. Aim for 5 seconds over goal pace, then settle.

Check the watch at the first mile marker, not before. GPS wanders in the first few hundred meters.

## A split plan for a 50:00 10K

- Mile 1: 8:08, controlled.
- Miles 2 to 5: 8:03 each, steady and a little hard.
- Mile 6: 8:03, hold on.
- Last 0.2 mile: whatever you have.

Write the numbers on your wrist or set the watch to show average pace for the current mile. Chasing the lap pace on a rolling course wastes effort, so hold the effort on hills and let the splits drift by 10 seconds.

## Use the last mile, not the first

If you reach mile 5 with energy, pick up the pace at mile 5.5 or 6. If you don't, hold the pace you have. A late push is a bonus, not part of the plan.

For music, a song in the right range keeps cadence steady. The [8:00 mile playlist](/music/pace/8-00-min-mile) and [10:00 mile playlist](/music/pace/10-00-min-mile) list tracks by pace, and the [music tool](/tools/music) matches any goal.

## Who this is wrong for

Even pacing is the default for runners who have trained 8 or more weeks and know their fitness. It is wrong for:

- First-time 10K runners with no recent 5K, who should run by effort and talk in short sentences.
- Hilly courses, where the effort rule beats the split rule.
- Runners racing for place, where the pack sets the pace.

A 10K under an hour needs no gels. If it runs past 75 minutes, see the [10K fuel plans](/fuel/plan/10k/1-15).
