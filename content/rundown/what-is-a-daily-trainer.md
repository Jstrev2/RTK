**Short answer:** A daily trainer is a durable, moderately cushioned shoe for easy runs and long runs. Most runners need one pair, and ours is the Brooks Ghost 18 ($150, 9.8 oz, 36 mm stack, 12 mm drop).

## A daily trainer is the shoe that takes most of your miles

Shoe makers sort models by job: daily trainer, recovery shoe, speed shoe, race shoe. The daily trainer is the middle one. It has enough foam for a long run, enough rubber to last hundreds of miles and no plate or racing geometry.

In our data, daily trainers sit at 34 to 41 mm of stack and weigh 9 to 10 oz. Anything lighter and firmer is for workouts. Anything with a much taller stack is a recovery or max-cushion shoe.

## One pair covers most runners

If you run three or four times a week at conversational pace, a single daily trainer does the whole job. A second pair starts to pay off at higher mileage, as covered in our [shoe rotation guide](/rundown/shoe-rotation-two-pairs).

These four are the safe picks in our database:

- [Brooks Ghost 18](/shoes/brooks-ghost-18): $150, 9.8 oz, 36 mm stack, 12 mm drop. Firm and dependable.
- [Nike Pegasus 42](/shoes/nike-pegasus-42): $145, 9.8 oz, 37 mm stack, 10 mm drop. Durable, accommodating fit.
- Saucony Ride 19: $145, 9.3 oz, 35 mm stack, 8 mm drop. The lightest of the four.
- ASICS Novablast 6: $150, 9.0 oz, 41 mm stack, 8 mm drop. Bouncier, and less stable.

## Pick by fit, then by feel

The four shoes are close enough that fit decides. A shoe that fits badly will not get worn, and the spec sheet does not change that. Our [guide for beginners](/rundown/best-running-shoes-for-beginners) covers fit in more detail.

After fit, choose on ride. The Ghost 18 is the firmest of the four. The Novablast 6 has the most bounce, and our data lists its platform as not the most stable.

## Who a daily trainer is wrong for

- Runners who race often: a lighter shoe helps on race day. See [how to choose your first racing shoe](/rundown/how-to-choose-your-first-racing-shoe).
- Runners who want maximum softness: the max-cushion models in our [guide](/rundown/max-cushion-running-shoes-who-they-help) have 43 mm or more of stack.
- Runners who need guide rails: see [stability vs neutral](/rundown/stability-shoes-vs-neutral-do-you-need-support).
