The half marathon sits in an awkward spot for fueling. It's long enough that running on empty hurts, and short enough that you don't need the full marathon routine. That's why runners either overdo it with six gels or take nothing and fade at mile 10.

My answer: take two gels if you finish in under two hours, three if you're out there longer, and skip them entirely if you're under about 80 minutes. This is wrong for you if you've never taken a gel in training. Practice first.

## The math is carbs per hour, not gels per race

Sports nutrition guidance, including recommendations summarized by the American College of Sports Medicine, points to roughly 30 to 60 grams of carbohydrate per hour for efforts that last between one and two and a half hours. Under about an hour, the benefit is small. Over two and a half hours, the ceiling climbs toward 90 grams for runners whose guts are trained for it.

A half marathon falls in the first band for almost everyone. So the question becomes how many grams you want and how many gels carry them.

The gels in our live table carry between 17 and 40 grams each. The common ones sit near 22 to 25. The [GU Original](/fuel/gels/gu-original-energy-gel) has 22 g, the [Maurten Gel 100](/fuel/gels/maurten-gel-100) has 25 g, and the [Torq Energy Gel](/fuel/gels/torq-energy-gel) and [Precision Fuel PF 30](/fuel/gels/precision-fuel-pf-30-gel) have 30 g each.

## What that means by finish time

Here's the plan I'd use, taking about 30 to 45 grams per hour and starting a bit before you feel you need it.

At a finish around 1:30 to 1:45, one gel at about 40 minutes and a second near 70 minutes is plenty. With 25 g gels that's 50 g across the race, which is about 30 to 35 g per hour. At 1:45 to 2:15, add a third around 100 minutes if you feel flat. Two or three gels is the range, and 2:00 is where I'd go with three if you've trained with them. Over 2:15 you're closer to a marathon-style plan, a gel every 40 to 45 minutes.

Under 80 minutes, you're running hard for a short time. Your body already holds enough glycogen, and the gain is small. Eat breakfast, maybe take one gel around the 40-minute mark if you want it, and rinse your mouth with a sports drink if it helps your head.

You can see a plan for your own time on our [half marathon fuel plans](/fuel/plan/half-marathon/2-00), and there are pages for [1:45](/fuel/plan/half-marathon/1-45) and [2:15](/fuel/plan/half-marathon/2-15). The [fueling tool](/tools/fueling) builds a schedule from your goal time.

## Pick a gel that fits your stomach, not your friend's

Carbs matter, but so does what else is in the gel:

- Caffeine: a few gels carry it, and it can help late in the race. The [caffeinated gels list](/fuel/best/caffeinated-gels) shows the range, from 25 mg up to 100 mg. If you're not used to caffeine, take half a dose or skip it.
- Sodium: our table runs from 0 to 245 mg per serving. On a hot day, a salty gel helps. On a cool day, you may not need to think about it.
- Concentration: thick gels like the 40 g Maurten Gel 160 need water. Thinner ones go down easier. Take any gel with a few sips of water, not a big gulp.

If you want one with more carbs per packet, the [30 g gels page](/fuel/best/30g-carb-gels) lists what's available.

## What to do in the last 10 minutes before the gun

Eat your usual breakfast two to three hours out. Take a gel at the start line only if you've done it in training. Don't try something new, because race day is not the time to find out a gel upsets your stomach.

If you've had stomach trouble in long runs before, our guide on [training your gut for race fueling](/rundown/train-your-gut-race-fueling) walks through how to build up to it. And if the gels you carry have gone sticky or warm, don't worry about it; taste matters less than tolerance.

## Start with two and adjust

If you're unsure, carry two gels and a third in your pocket. Take the first at about 40 minutes and the second at 70. If you feel good at 100, you don't need the third. If you feel flat, that's your cue.
