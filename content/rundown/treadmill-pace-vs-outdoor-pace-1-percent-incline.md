**Short answer:** Set the treadmill to 0 or 1% and run by effort, not by the number on the belt. The 1% rule dates to 1996, and a 2015 study found even 1% felt harder than outdoors.

## The 1% rule came from one small study

Jones and Doust (1996, Journal of Sports Sciences) had nine trained men run on a treadmill and outdoors on a level road. At 1% the oxygen cost matched the road for runs of about 5 minutes, at speeds from 2.92 to 5.0 metres per second.

The reason is air resistance. Indoors there is none, so running costs less at the same speed, and a slight grade adds the cost back. Nine runners and 5-minute efforts is a small base for a rule most gyms repeat.

## Later work found the treadmill costs more, not the same

Mooses and colleagues (2015) tested 13 competitive distance runners with the treadmill at 1%. Running economy was 215.4 ml of oxygen per kg per km on the track and 236.8 on the treadmill. That is about 10% higher on the treadmill, the opposite of what the 1% rule predicts.

The two studies used different runners, different belts and different speeds. Treat 1% as a rough match at best.

## What to set

- Easy runs: 0 or 1%. Pace matters little, so run by breathing and the talk test.
- Tempo and race-pace work: start at 1%, hold the effort you'd hold outdoors, and accept a belt pace that may be a bit slower.
- Hill workouts: set the grade you want and run it by effort.
- Speed shown by the belt is no race predictor. Use a recent race on the [pace calculator](/tools/pace-calculator) to set targets.

## Why belt pace feels different

Belts vary, and many treadmills read a bit fast or slow. Indoor heat adds strain, so heart rate climbs at the same pace. A fan helps, and so does a [playlist matched to your pace](/tools/music), such as the [9:00 mile list](/music/pace/9-00-min-mile).

If your goal pace is on the belt for a long run, you can drop the pace a few seconds a mile and keep the effort. That is a better rule than any incline.

## Shoes and cadence indoors

Cadence is easy to read on a belt, and the [cadence guide](/rundown/how-to-find-your-running-cadence) shows how to count it. A softer trainer is fine on a belt, and the [shoe selector](/tools/shoe-selector) can filter by cushion. Two pairs in a [rotation](/rundown/shoe-rotation-two-pairs) last longer than one.
