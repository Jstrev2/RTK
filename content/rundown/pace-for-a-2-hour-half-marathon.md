**Short answer:** A 2:00 half marathon is 9:09 per mile, or 5:41 per kilometer. A 10K run in about 59:20 predicts it.

## The split table

The half marathon is 13.1094 miles. Running 9:09 a mile gives 1:59:57, so you have 3 seconds to spare. Run the first mile at 9:09, not faster.

- 5K: 28:26 at even pace.
- 10K: 56:51 at even pace.
- 10 miles: 1:31:30.
- 13.1 miles: 1:59:57.

The [pace calculator](/tools/pace-calculator) builds a split card for any goal.

## A 10K near 59:20 predicts a 2-hour half

The Riegel formula (1981, American Scientist) estimates race times as time multiplied by the distance ratio raised to the power 1.06. It puts a 2:00 half at 59:20 for 10K and 30:51 for 5K.

It's an estimate, not a promise. It assumes you've trained for the longer distance, and a runner with a fast 10K and a short long run will usually finish slower than it predicts.

## Pace it even, with a slow first mile

Plan 9:15 for mile 1 and 9:09 after. A crowded first mile slows most runners anyway, so don't chase the lost seconds.

- Miles 1 to 3: 9:10 to 9:15, controlled.
- Miles 4 to 10: 9:09, steady.
- Miles 11 to 13: hold 9:09, or push if you have energy.

## Check the goal against recent races

Use a recent race as the starting point, not a hope. A 10K at 59:20 or faster in the last two months supports the goal, and a 10K near 62:00 does not, so the 2:00 plan moves to a later race. Rerun the [pace calculator](/tools/pace-calculator) with each new result.

If the numbers point to 2:05 instead, the pace is 9:32 a mile. A 2:10 half is 9:55. Pick the target your races support and train for that one.

## Fuel and shoes for two hours

Two hours is long enough to need carbs. Our [half marathon gel guide](/rundown/how-many-gels-for-a-half-marathon) puts the plan at two or three gels, and the [2:00 half plan](/fuel/plan/half-marathon/2-00) lists them by mile.
