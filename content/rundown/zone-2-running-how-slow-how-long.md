**Short answer:** Zone 2 is the pace where you can speak in full sentences, usually 60 to 75% of max heart rate. Most of your runs should sit there, for 30 to 60 minutes at a time.

## The talk test finds zone 2 without a heart rate monitor

Persinger and colleagues tested this in 2004 (Medicine and Science in Sports and Exercise). They found the talk test approximates ventilatory threshold on both treadmill and bike, and that once speech was no longer comfortable the intensity was above threshold.

So the rule is plain. If you can say a few sentences in a row without a gasp, you're in the zone. If you can only manage single words, you're above it.

## Heart rate gives a range, not a number

Commonly quoted zone 2 ranges run from 60 to 75% of max heart rate. Those are estimates, and age formulas for max heart rate can miss by a wide margin. A lab lactate test is the accurate way to set zones.

Heat, poor sleep and dehydration all raise heart rate at the same pace. On those days, hold the talk test and let the pace drop.

## It will feel too slow, and that's normal

Zone 2 often sits slower than the pace runners jog at by habit. Walking breaks on hills are fine. Slowing down is the skill.

Seiler's 2010 review of elite endurance athletes (International Journal of Sports Physiology and Performance) found most of their sessions were at low intensity, around 80%.

## How much to run there

- New runners: all of it, 3 runs a week of 20 to 40 minutes.
- Regular runners: 3 or 4 runs a week at 30 to 60 minutes, with one or two harder sessions.
- Long run: zone 2 for the whole run, including in marathon training.

Check your cadence on these runs with the [cadence guide](/rundown/how-to-find-your-running-cadence). The [music tool](/tools/music) can set a playlist to a slow pace, and [slow songs help keep easy runs easy](/music).

## Who this is wrong for

Zone 2 is not a race-day plan. Racing takes harder work, and a runner with a 5K next week needs sessions at race pace too. Use the [pace calculator](/tools/pace-calculator) to find race pace, and keep the easy runs easy.
