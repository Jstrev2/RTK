**Short answer:** Buy the Endorphin Speed 6 unless you are racing a marathon for time. It costs $55 less than the Pro 5 and works for tempos, long runs and races.

## The Pro 5 is $55 more and 0.9 oz lighter

- [Saucony Endorphin Speed 6](/shoes/saucony-endorphin-speed-6): $175, 8.2 oz (men's), 7 oz (women's), 38 mm stack, 8 mm drop, nylon plate, moderate cushion.
- [Saucony Endorphin Pro 5](/shoes/saucony-endorphin-pro-5): $230, 7.3 oz (men's), 6.4 oz (women's), 39 mm stack, 8 mm drop, carbon plate, maximum cushion.

Our data gives the Pro 5 a narrow toe box and the Speed 6 a standard one. That matters more than the weight if your feet are wide.

## The Speed 6 does more jobs

Our data lists the Speed 6 for speed work, long runs and race day. It uses PWRRUN PB foam and a nylon plate, which gives some snap without carbon stiffness. Our data calls it durable enough for regular training.

Choose it if you want one fast shoe for the whole week. The cost is the price creeping up, and a fit that runs slightly short, so consider sizing up a half size.

## The Pro 5 is for race day

The Pro 5 adds a carbon plate, a tall max-cushion stack and a rocker that suits marathon pace. Our data lists it for race day and speed work only. The downsides are the $230 price and a narrow fit that demands a settled stride.

Research on carbon-plated shoes is mixed on who benefits. The best-known study found a 4% average running economy gain in 18 male runners who had run a 10K in under 31 minutes (Hoogkamer and colleagues, 2018, Sports Medicine). It was Nike funded and tested a Nike shoe, so it says little about a slower runner in a Saucony.

## Who each one is wrong for

- Speed 6: wrong for runners who want a max-stack, carbon-plated racer.
- Pro 5: wrong for wide feet, for daily training and for anyone watching the budget.
