**Short answer:** Plan on 60 grams of carbs an hour from 30 minutes in. With 25-gram gels that's 7 gels for a 3:30 marathon, 8 for 4:00 and 11 for 5:00.

## Count grams first, then divide by your gel

The gels in our [gel table](/fuel) carry 17 to 40 grams of carbs each, so the number of packets depends on which gel you carry. Start from grams per hour and divide.

60 grams an hour is the usual target for races of two to three hours (Jeukendrup, Sports Medicine, 2014). Runners out for four hours or more can build toward 90 grams an hour, but only with gels that mix glucose and fructose, and only after practicing it.

## Gels by finish time

These counts start at 30 minutes, keep 60 grams an hour and stop 20 minutes from the finish.

- 3:00: 6 gels of 25 g, or 6 of 22 g
- 3:30: 7 gels of 25 g, or 8 of 22 g
- 4:00: 8 gels of 25 g, or 9 of 22 g
- 4:30: 9 gels of 25 g, or 11 of 22 g
- 5:00: 11 gels of 25 g, or 12 of 22 g

The [Maurten Gel 100](/fuel/gels/maurten-gel-100) is 25 g, the [GU Energy Gel](/fuel/gels/gu-original-energy-gel) 23 g and the [SiS GO Isotonic](/fuel/gels/sis-go-isotonic-energy-gel) 22 g. A 30 g gel such as the [Precision Fuel PF 30](/fuel/gels/precision-fuel-pf-30-gel) brings a 4:00 marathon down to 7 packets.

Sports drink from the course counts too. Look up the drink's carbs per cup on the race website and skip one gel for every 25 grams you expect to drink.

## First gel at 30 minutes, then on a clock

- Take the first gel about 30 minutes in, before you feel hungry.
- Space the rest evenly: every 25 minutes with 25 g gels, every 22 minutes with 22 g gels, every 30 minutes with 30 g gels.
- Take each one at an aid station with a few sips of water.
- Skip the last 20 minutes. A gel that late does little before the finish.
- Set watch alerts or write the times on your arm. Late gels are the most common way a plan falls apart.

For a schedule with times and miles filled in, open the [4:00 marathon fuel plan](/fuel/plan/marathon/4-00) or enter your own goal in the [Fuel Finder](/tools/fueling). Both adjust the target for your experience, so their counts can run a gel or two higher.

## The count only works if you've practiced it

A stomach that has never taken 60 grams an hour at marathon pace often refuses it on race day. Build up over six to eight weeks of long runs, using the exact gel you'll race with. [Training your gut for race fueling](/rundown/train-your-gut-race-fueling) has the week-by-week build.

This plan is wrong for two groups:

- Half marathoners and 10K runners. Two gels at most cover a half, and a 10K needs none.
- Runners with diabetes or another condition that changes how you handle carbs. Set the target with your doctor or a dietitian.
