**Short answer:** Buy a wide-based, durable max cushion trainer. The Brooks Ghost Max 4 is $160 and 10.3 oz, and our data says its stable-neutral base suits heavier runners.

## Look for width and durable foam

Our shoe notes for heavier runners come back to a wide base, durable foam and a sturdy outsole.

Weight does matter. Our data warns that the Salomon Aero Blaze 3 at $140 has a "midsole that compresses under heavier runners". The Mizuno Neo Zen 2 lists "foam durability trails firmer trainers".

## The Ghost Max 4 is the default pick

The [Brooks Ghost Max 4](/shoes/brooks-ghost-max-4) is $160 with a 39 mm stack, 6 mm drop and a rocker. Its limits are weight and pace: 10.3 oz, and our data calls it too heavy for workouts.

Treat it as the easy-day and long-run shoe. Fast days need a lighter pair.

## Other picks with data behind them

- [Saucony Hurricane 25](/shoes/saucony-hurricane-25): $160, 10.7 oz, 38 mm, moderate stability, "durable enough for heavier runners".
- [Hoka Gaviota 6](/shoes/hoka-gaviota-6): $175, 10.9 oz, 39 mm, moderate stability, "great for bigger runners and long easy days".
- Diadora Cellula 2: $185, 10.9 oz, 38 mm, very protective and stable, and it runs short.
- Saucony Procyon Max: $120, 10.7 oz, 38 mm, the budget option with a broad base.

## Choose Hurricane 25 over Ghost Max 4 if you want more guidance

The [Saucony Hurricane 25](/shoes/saucony-hurricane-25) has moderate stability where the Ghost Max 4 is rated mild. Both are $160 and within 0.4 oz of each other. Pick the Hurricane if your ankles roll inward in your current shoes, and the Ghost Max 4 if you want a neutral feel with a wide base.

Skip very soft, tall shoes known for wobble, such as the Novablast 6, whose data lists "not the most stable platform".

## Check for packed-down foam early

Soft foam can compress under more load, as the Aero Blaze 3 note shows. If a shoe feels flat sooner than expected, the signs are in our [shoe lifespan guide](/rundown/how-long-do-running-shoes-last).

## Who this advice is wrong for

- Runners who want a light, fast shoe. Every pick above is over 10 oz, and none fits a tempo day.
- Runners who need a heavy stability shoe. Our [stability and neutral guide](/rundown/stability-shoes-vs-neutral-do-you-need-support) covers who benefits.
- Anyone buying by weight alone. Try shoes on, and filter further with the [shoe selector](/tools/shoe-selector) or the [list for heavy runners](/shoes/best/running-shoes-for-heavy-runners).
