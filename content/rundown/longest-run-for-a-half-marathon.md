**Short answer:** Peak at 10 to 12 miles, and stop at about 2 hours on your feet if you're slower than 10:00 a mile. The race supplies the last 1 to 3 miles.

## Ten miles is the standard peak

Hal Higdon's Half Marathon Novice 1 plan tops out at a 10-mile long run, according to its TrainingPeaks listing, and it averages 15 miles a week. That plan is the most common template for a first half, and it finishes people.

Runners with a time goal can go to 11 or 12 miles. The extra miles build confidence in pace more than fitness, so they're optional.

## Slower runners should cap by time, not miles

Miles cost more at a slow pace. The same distance takes longer, which means more hours of pounding and more fuel. A time cap keeps the load similar across paces.

- 8:00 a mile: 12 miles takes 1:36.
- 10:00 a mile: 12 miles takes 2:00.
- 12:00 a mile: 10 miles takes 2:00, so stop there.
- 14:00 a mile: 9 miles takes 2:06, so stop there.

These caps are our planning ranges, not a research result. Run the long run at an easy pace, 1 to 2 minutes a mile slower than race pace. The [pace calculator](/tools/pace-calculator) gives your race pace for any goal.

## Build the long run by 1 mile a week

Add 1 mile every week or two and drop back by 2 to 3 miles every fourth week. Start from the longest run you've done in the last month, not from the plan's number. If that's 5 miles, a 10-mile peak is a 5 to 8 week build.

Hold the peak for one or two runs, then cut the distance in the last 2 weeks.

## Fuel the long run like the race

Past 90 minutes, runs need carbs, and the long run is where you test them. Our [half marathon gel guide](/rundown/how-many-gels-for-a-half-marathon) sets the count by finish time, and [race day fueling mistakes](/rundown/race-day-fueling-mistakes) covers what goes wrong when the first gel comes on race day.

Practice the gel timing at your peak run. Two runs of 10 miles or more give you two tries.

## Who this is wrong for

The plan above is for healthy runners building from a base of 10 or more miles a week. It is wrong for:

- Runners under 10 miles a week, who need a base block first. The [weekly mileage guide](/rundown/how-many-miles-a-week-to-train-for-a-marathon) covers the build rate.
- Anyone in pain. See a doctor or physical therapist before adding miles.
