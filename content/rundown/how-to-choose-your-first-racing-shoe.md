**Short answer:** Buy a racing shoe once you're training for a time, not just a finish. The Saucony Endorphin Speed 6 ($175) is the forgiving first pick; carbon super shoes start at $250.

## You need one only when the time matters

If you're running your first half or marathon to finish, your daily trainer is the right shoe. Racing shoes give up stability and comfort for speed, and tired, untrained legs feel that trade.

If you're chasing a time, a plated shoe can help. In a 2018 lab study, a Nike prototype improved running economy by 4% on average over two other marathon shoes (Hoogkamer and colleagues). The test ran 18 trained runners at 14 to 18 km/h, about 6:54 to 5:22 per mile, so the effect at slower paces is less certain.

## What makes a racing shoe different

- A stiff plate in the midsole: carbon, or nylon in gentler shoes
- Light foam that returns more energy than a trainer's EVA
- A rocker shape that rolls you forward through each stride
- Less stability and a thinner outsole than a daily trainer

That last point matters for buying. Racing shoes wear faster and cost more, so most runners keep them for races and a few key workouts.

## Four racing shoes from our data

- [Saucony Endorphin Speed 6](/shoes/saucony-endorphin-speed-6), $175, 8.2 oz. A nylon plate makes it more forgiving than carbon, and it's durable enough for regular training. Our pick for a first fast shoe. It fits slightly short, so consider a half size up.
- [Nike Vaporfly 4](/shoes/nike-vaporfly-4), $260, 6.0 oz. The lightest of the four and good across race distances. Narrow fit and a short-lived outsole.
- [Adidas Adizero Adios Pro 5](/shoes/adidas-adizero-adios-pro-5), $250, 7.6 oz. Carbon rods instead of a plate and an aggressive forward roll. Best for midfoot and forefoot strikers.
- [Hoka Rocket X 3](/shoes/hoka-rocket-x-3), $250, 7.4 oz. A winged carbon plate makes it unusually stable for a super shoe. The pick if other racers feel wobbly.

## Match it to the race and your legs

For a 5K or 10K, lighter and firmer works. For a marathon, take more cushion: your legs need help at mile 22, not mile 2.

Run at least two or three tempo runs or long runs in the shoe before race day. Calves and feet need time to adjust to a plate and a rocker. Never debut a racing shoe on race day.
