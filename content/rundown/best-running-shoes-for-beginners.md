**Short answer:** Buy a neutral daily trainer that fits the day you try it on. The Brooks Ghost 18 ($150) is our default, the Nike Winflo 12 ($110) the cheaper pick and the Hoka Clifton 11 ($150) the softer one.

## Fit decides it, because the specs barely differ

Beginner trainers sit in a narrow band. The Brooks Ghost 18 has a 36 mm stack and a 12 mm drop, and the [Nike Pegasus 42](/shoes/nike-pegasus-42) has 37 mm and 10 mm. A new runner won't feel that gap, but a pinched toe shows up in the first mile.

Check these in the store or in the first ten minutes at home:

- A thumb's width of room past your longest toe
- No heel slip when you walk fast or jog
- Nothing rubs or presses anywhere
- Tried on in the afternoon, when feet are bigger, with the socks you'll run in

A shoe that feels almost right on day one feels wrong by mile four. Keep the box and the receipt until you've run in it.

## Three first shoes worth buying

- [Brooks Ghost 18](/shoes/brooks-ghost-18), $150, 9.8 oz. Neutral, moderate cushion, a durable outsole and wide sizes. It plays it safe: firmer and less bouncy than max-cushion shoes.
- [Nike Winflo 12](/shoes/nike-winflo-12), $110, 9.7 oz. Firmer foam and a durable outsole. Not built for workouts, which doesn't matter for three easy runs a week.
- [Hoka Clifton 11](/shoes/hoka-clifton-11), $150, 9.7 oz, 42 mm stack. The soft one, comfortable for walking and standing too. It feels sluggish at faster paces.

Try on the Ghost and the Clifton side by side and buy the one your feet stop noticing. If $150 is too much, the Winflo is the pick, and [our under-$120 guide](/rundown/good-running-shoes-under-120) has four more.

## What a first pair can skip

- Carbon-plated racers. They're built for race day and cost $250 and up. [Choosing your first racing shoe](/rundown/how-to-choose-your-first-racing-shoe) covers when one makes sense.
- Stability shoes, unless a neutral shoe has given you a reason. [Stability vs neutral](/rundown/stability-shoes-vs-neutral-do-you-need-support) explains who needs support.
- The lightest shoe on the wall. At 9.7 to 9.8 oz, all three picks are light enough for easy running.

## Your second pair will be a better buy

After a month of running you'll know whether you like soft or firm, roomy or snug. That makes the second purchase easier than the first. The [Shoe Finder](/tools/shoe-selector) narrows the list by your foot, your runs and your budget in a couple of minutes.
