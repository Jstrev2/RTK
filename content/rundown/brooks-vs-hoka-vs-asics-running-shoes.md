**Short answer:** Brooks is the safe, firmer choice with a 10 to 12 mm drop. Hoka is the tallest and softest, ASICS is the middle option at 8 mm drop, and each brand's flagship daily trainer costs $145 to $150.

## The three flagships cost the same and weigh within 0.6 oz

Each brand has one daily trainer that most runners buy first. Numbers are from our shoe data, men's sizes.

- [Brooks Ghost 18](/shoes/brooks-ghost-18): $150, 9.8 oz, 36 mm stack, 12 mm drop
- [Hoka Clifton 11](/shoes/hoka-clifton-11): $150, 9.7 oz, 42 mm stack, 8 mm drop
- [ASICS Gel-Cumulus 28](/shoes/asics-gel-cumulus-28): $145, 9.2 oz, 38 mm stack, 8 mm drop

Price and weight do not separate them. Stack and drop do.

## Brooks is the firmest and highest drop

The Ghost 18 has the shortest stack of the three and a 12 mm drop. Our data lists it as dependable, durable and a fit for most feet, with wide sizes available. Its weakness is little energy return.

The plush Brooks is the Glycerin 23 at $165 and 10.1 oz, still at a 10 mm drop. Stability runners get the Adrenaline GTS 26 at $155.

## Hoka is the tallest and softest

The Clifton 11 stands 42 mm tall with a rocker shape, and the Bondi 9 goes to 43 mm at $170 and 10.6 oz. Hoka drops are lower: 8 mm on the Clifton and 5 mm on the Bondi.

The cost is versatility. Both are heavy and sluggish above easy pace. The lighter Hoka is the [Mach 7](/shoes/hoka-mach-7) at $145 and 8.3 oz, a daily trainer that handles tempo runs.

## ASICS sits in the middle and runs 8 mm across the range

The Cumulus 28, the [Novablast 6](/shoes/asics-novablast-6) and the Gel-Nimbus 29 all have an 8 mm drop. That makes switching between ASICS models easy on the calves.

- Cumulus 28: the plain daily trainer, light for its cushion
- Novablast 6: $150, 9.0 oz, 41 mm stack, the bouncy one
- Nimbus 29: $170, 9.8 oz, 43 mm stack, the plush one

## Pick by drop and softness

Buy Brooks if you want a firm, protective trainer and run fine at 10 to 12 mm drop. Buy Hoka if softness and standing comfort matter most. Buy ASICS if you want a lighter shoe or plan to own more than one model.

Brooks is wrong for runners who want a soft, tall ride. Hoka is wrong for anyone who wants one shoe that also does workouts. ASICS is wrong for runners who want a roomy toe box, since most of its range is standard width.
