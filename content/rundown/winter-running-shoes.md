**Short answer:** For cold roads, buy a shoe with proven wet grip, since winter is wet. The Puma Velocity Nitro 5 is $140 and 8.1 oz, with "great grip in the wet".

## Our data flags one shoe for cold foam

The [New Balance Fresh Foam 680 v8](/shoes/new-balance-fresh-foam-680-v8) is $80, 9.5 oz, 35 mm. Our notes say the midsole "firms up noticeably in cold weather". It is a sensible first running shoe, so it still fits a budget in mild weather.

The Ellipse and Velocity Nitro 5 notes list no cold-weather issue, though that is a gap in our notes and not a test result. We have not tested cold foam shoe by shoe, so the data flags this one only. If a shoe feels hard on a cold morning, give it the first mile to settle.

## Pick grip first

Rain and slush matter more than temperature for most winter runs, and wet pavement is where weak rubber slips. Check the outsole notes before the foam notes. Shoes our notes list as good in the wet:

- [Puma Velocity Nitro 5](/shoes/puma-velocity-nitro-5): $140, 8.1 oz, 37 mm.
- [New Balance Ellipse](/shoes/new-balance-ellipse): $145, 9.6 oz, 38 mm.
- Craft Nordlite Tempo: $180, 9.5 oz, 40 mm, with "excellent wet-road grip". The fit runs narrow and slightly small, so it suits runners who already know they fit Craft.

The [rain shoes guide](/rundown/running-shoes-for-rain) lists four to skip.

## The Ellipse is the pick for slow winter miles

It costs $5 more than the Velocity Nitro 5 and has a taller 38 mm stack. At 9.6 oz it is heavier, which does not matter at easy pace. Choose the Velocity Nitro 5 if you do faster runs in the cold.

## Who this is wrong for

- Runners on snow or ice. Our data lists no shoe with snow or ice traction, and these are road shoes.
- Anyone with one good daily pair. Keep it, and add a [second pair for rotation](/rundown/shoe-rotation-two-pairs) only if wet days wear it out.
- Runners shopping on price alone. Check the [last year's model guide](/rundown/last-years-running-shoe-model-what-changes) for savings.
