**Short answer:** A super trainer pairs a tall stack of race-style foam with trainer durability. Most runners should stay in a regular daily trainer, and the Adizero Evo SL ($150, 7.9 oz) is the value entry if you want one.

## A super trainer sits between the daily trainer and the racer

The category covers shoes with 39 to 46 mm of stack and soft, high-rebound foam that also hold up to daily mileage. Some have a plate and some don't. They weigh 8 to 10 oz, lighter than most daily trainers, and cost $150 to $200.

Race shoes are lighter and cost more, and their outsoles are not built for a full training block.

## Plated and plateless versions both exist

- [Adidas Adizero Evo SL](/shoes/adidas-adizero-evo-sl): $150, 7.9 oz, 39 mm stack, 6.5 mm drop. No plate. Thin outsole coverage wears faster.
- [ASICS Superblast 3](/shoes/asics-superblast-3): $200, 8.8 oz, 45 mm stack, 8 mm drop. No plate. Our data calls it overkill for short easy runs.
- [Hoka Mach X 3](/shoes/hoka-mach-x-3): $190, 9.3 oz, 44 mm stack, 5 mm drop. Plated, and more stable than most plated trainers.
- [Puma Deviate Nitro 4](/shoes/puma-deviate-nitro-4): $160, 9.2 oz, 39 mm stack, 10 mm drop. Plated, good value for the category.
- [Brooks Hyperion Max 4](/shoes/brooks-hyperion-max-4): $180, 8.2 oz, 36 mm stack, 6 mm drop. Plated, firmer ride.

## A plate is not what makes the shoe

Foam, stack and weight decide most of the ride. Our data describes the Mach X 3 plate as acting more like a stabilizer than a propulsion element. For more on what plates do for slower runners, see [carbon plates for non-elite runners](/rundown/carbon-plate-shoes-for-non-elite-runners).

## Who should buy one

A super trainer makes sense for runners doing three or more runs a week with a weekly long run and tempo work, who want one shoe for all of it. It also suits runners whose goal race is a marathon and who want the race shoe's feel in training without wearing it out.

It is wrong for beginners, who get more from a [regular daily trainer](/rundown/what-is-a-daily-trainer), and for runners on a budget. A $200 shoe on easy 3-mile days is a lot of price for a small gain.

The Evo SL is the pick if price matters. The Superblast 3 is the pick if you want the most cushion.
