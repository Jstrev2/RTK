**Short answer:** Buy last year's model if it's cheaper. Across 10 version updates in our data, weight moved 0.1 oz or less and drop never changed.

## Most updates are small

We compared the latest version with the one before it for daily trainers with two generations in our data:

- [Brooks Ghost 17](/shoes/brooks-ghost-17) to [Ghost 18](/shoes/brooks-ghost-18): $150 both, 9.8 oz both, 36 mm stack, 12 mm drop.
- [Hoka Clifton 10](/shoes/hoka-clifton-10) to [Clifton 11](/shoes/hoka-clifton-11): $150 both, 9.8 oz to 9.7 oz, 42 mm, 8 mm.
- ASICS Gel-Cumulus 27 to Cumulus 28: $145 both, 9.3 oz to 9.2 oz, 38 mm, 8 mm.
- Saucony Triumph 23 to Triumph 24: $170 both, 9.8 oz to 9.7 oz, 37 mm, 10 mm.
- ASICS Gel-Nimbus 27 to Nimbus 28: $165 both, 10.7 oz to 10.6 oz, 43 mm, 8 mm.

The exceptions are price rises: the Hoka Mach 6 at $140 became the [Mach 7](/shoes/hoka-mach-7) at $145, and the Ghost 16 at $140 became the Ghost 17 at $150.

## Our notes call several updates modest

The same shoes carry blunt notes. The Pegasus 42 is an "incremental update over the 41", the Glycerin 23 a "small update if you own the 22", and the Ghost Max 4 a "modest update over the Max 3". Our full write-up of one case is in the [Pegasus 42 vs 41 guide](/rundown/nike-pegasus-42-vs-41).

## Buy the old version when it costs less

Our data lists launch prices and does not track sales, so check the retailer's price for both versions. If last year's pair is under the new list price, buy it. A $20 gap on a $150 shoe is about 13 percent.

Check the release date in our shoe data too. The next version often lands about a year after the last, so a shoe released more than 11 months ago is likely due for a replacement.

## Pay for the new one when the foam changed

Some updates swap the midsole, and then weight and price hide the real difference. Our Kinvara 16 note says "low energy return from dated foam", which tells you the foam did not move on. Read the pros and cons on the new shoe's page. If the headline pro is a new foam, the update is worth paying for.

## Who this advice is wrong for

- Runners who wore the old version and liked it. Buy a second pair of it now, before stock disappears.
- Anyone with a fit problem in the old version that the update fixed. Our Arahi 9 note says "wider, roomier forefoot fit" over the Arahi 8.
- Runners with a race coming up. Do not change shoes inside two weeks of it.
