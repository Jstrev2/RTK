**Short answer:** No. Water is enough for most runs of an hour or less. Electrolytes start to earn their place on runs over 90 minutes, in hot weather, or if you sweat heavily.

## Under an hour, water covers it

The American College of Sports Medicine's 1996 position stand on exercise and fluid replacement found little evidence of a difference in performance between a carbohydrate-electrolyte drink and plain water during exercise under one hour. It recommended adding sodium for exercise lasting longer than an hour. That statement is old, and the 2007 update asked for fluid plans tailored to each runner.

Eat a normal meal, drink to thirst and run. An electrolyte tablet on an easy hour is a purchase, not a training aid.

## Past 90 minutes, sodium becomes a choice

Our 90-minute line is a planning rule, not a study threshold. Past it, sweat losses add up, and a drink with sodium makes drinking easier. The numbers from our [electrolyte list](/fuel/best/electrolytes):

- [Nuun Sport](/fuel/gels/nuun-sport) tablet: 300 mg sodium.
- GU Hydration Drink Tabs: 320 mg.
- SaltStick Electrolyte Capsules: 215 mg per capsule.
- [Precision Fuel PH 1000](/fuel/gels/precision-fuel-ph-1000-tablets): 500 mg per tablet in 500 ml.

A gel often covers part of it. The [GU Roctane Energy Gel](/fuel/gels/gu-roctane-energy-gel-caffeinated) has 125 mg of sodium and the PowerBar PowerGel (Caffeinated) has 210 mg, so a long run with gels may need no tablet. Our [high-sodium gel list](/fuel/best/high-sodium-gels) ranks the rest.

## Heat and heavy sweat change the answer

In hot weather the one-hour rule bends. If your shirt dries with white streaks, or you lose weight on the scale after a run, you're losing more salt than average.

- Hot, humid run of 60 to 90 minutes: one tablet in the bottle is reasonable.
- Cool run under 60 minutes: water only.
- Long run over 90 minutes: sodium from a gel, a drink or a tablet.

Drinking far more than thirst asks for is the other risk. Over-drinking water on a long run can dilute blood sodium, so sip, don't force.

## Cramps are not a reason to buy salt

Muscle cramps get blamed on salt, and a tablet is an easy purchase. We have no data showing it prevents them. If you cramp often, see a doctor or physical therapist.
