**Short answer:** Buy the Vaporfly 4 if you race anything from 5K to the marathon, since it is $25 cheaper and 1.4 oz lighter. Buy the Alphafly 4 only for marathon-focused racing with the most cushion.

## The Alphafly is taller, heavier and $25 more

- [Nike Vaporfly 4](/shoes/nike-vaporfly-4): $260, 6 oz (men's), 5.1 oz (women's), 39 mm stack, 6 mm drop, narrow toe box.
- [Nike Alphafly 4](/shoes/nike-alphafly-4): $285, 7.4 oz (men's), 6.4 oz (women's), 40 mm stack, 8 mm drop, standard toe box.

Both have ZoomX foam and a full carbon plate. The Alphafly adds forefoot Air Zoom units and sits at the 40 mm legal limit.

## The Vaporfly 4 is the versatile one

Our data calls it the most versatile super shoe Nike makes, at home from 5K to the marathon and light enough for hard workouts. At 6 oz it is among the lightest shoes in our database.

The trade-offs are the narrow fit, which will not suit wide feet, and limited outsole durability. Save it for race day and a few key sessions.

## The Alphafly 4 is the marathon one

Our data lists the Alphafly 4 for race day only, 10K to the marathon. It has maximum cushion and an aggressive, bouncy push-off. It is not durable enough for regular training, and the price is $285.

It has a standard toe box, so it is the better fit of the two for feet that find the Vaporfly narrow. The [Alphafly 3 vs 4](/shoes/compare/nike-alphafly-3-vs-nike-alphafly-4) page shows the changes from last year.

## Super shoes help, by about 4% in the best study

Hoogkamer and colleagues (2018, Sports Medicine) measured a 4% average improvement in running economy, ranging from 2 to 6%, in 18 male runners who had each run a 10K in under 31 minutes. Nike funded it and the shoe was a Vaporfly prototype. A 4% economy gain does not mean a 4% faster race, and the study did not test slower runners.

## Who each one is wrong for

- Vaporfly 4: wrong for wide feet and for runners who want maximum cushion over 26.2 miles.
- Alphafly 4: wrong for 5K racing, for training runs and for anyone who prefers a light shoe.

If you have never raced in a plated shoe, read [how to choose your first racing shoe](/rundown/how-to-choose-your-first-racing-shoe). Set a target with the [pace calculator](/tools/pace-calculator) before you decide a $260 shoe is worth it.
