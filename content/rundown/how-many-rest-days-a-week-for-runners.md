**Short answer:** Take 1 to 2 full days off a week if you run 5 or 6 days. Run 3 or 4 days and you already have 3 or 4 days off.

## Rest days by how often you run

These are our planning ranges, not research results. We found no trial that fixes an ideal number of rest days for all runners.

- 3 runs a week: 4 days off, with easy cross-training or walking on up to 2 of them.
- 4 runs a week: 3 days off, never more than 2 runs in a row.
- 5 runs a week: 2 days off, or 1 day off and 1 very easy run.
- 6 runs a week: 1 day off, and 2 of the 6 runs at easy pace.
- 7 runs a week: no one needs this. Take a day off.

## A rest day is not an easy day

An easy day is a short run at a pace where you can talk in full sentences. A rest day is no running at all. Both help, and they do different things.

Easy days keep the weekly mileage up without adding much stress. Rest days give the legs a day to adapt. If every day is a hard-effort day, there is no recovery, and fitness stalls. The [zone 2 guide](/rundown/zone-2-running-how-slow-how-long) explains how slow an easy day is.

## Put rest before the hard days

Place the rest day the day before your hardest run, usually the long run or the workout.

- Hard run on Saturday: rest Friday.
- Workout on Tuesday and long run on Sunday: rest Monday and Friday.
- Never stack two hard runs back to back unless a plan tells you to.

The [weekly mileage guide](/rundown/how-many-miles-a-week-to-train-for-a-marathon) shows how mileage builds around these days.

## Add a day off when the signs show

Take an extra day off after a bad night of sleep, a race or a run that left you flat for two days. Skipping one run costs almost nothing. Pushing through can cost weeks.

Sharp or lasting pain is different from tired legs. See a doctor or physical therapist, and stop running until you have.

## Who should ignore these ranges

Runners following a coach's plan should follow the plan. Runners building from zero should start with a run-walk schedule, 3 days a week, and add a fourth day only after 4 weeks.
