**Short answer:** Count how many times your right foot lands in 30 seconds and multiply by four. Pick songs at that BPM, and if you want a higher cadence, raise it 3 to 5% at a time.

## Count 30 seconds of steps

- Warm up for ten minutes at your usual easy pace.
- On a flat stretch, count your right foot landing for 30 seconds.
- Multiply by four. That's steps per minute, which is what cadence means.
- Repeat on another day and at a faster pace.

Cadence rises as you speed up. An easy run in the 160s can turn into the 170s or 180s on a tempo run, and good runners fall outside both ranges. If your watch reports cadence, check it against one hand count to be sure it reads steps, not strides.

## Small changes are the only ones worth making

A 2011 lab study (Heiderscheit and colleagues, University of Wisconsin) ran 45 recreational runners at their normal step rate and at 5% and 10% above and below it. A 5% rise cut the load the knee absorbed. A 10% rise cut hip load too.

The authors also noted that a big change from your natural rate is hard to hold. It was one lab study at a fixed speed, so it doesn't set a target. From a natural 158, a goal of 166 is a reasonable experiment, and 180 is a different way of running.

## Songs at your cadence feel like they run with you

A track at 165 BPM fits a runner at 165 steps a minute. That's why a playlist matched to your number works better than a random shuffle. The [Music Finder](/tools/music) builds one from a BPM or a pace, and the [easy run playlist](/music/workout/easy-run) is a ready-made starting point.

Half-time songs double the choice. A track at 85 BPM matches 170 steps if one foot lands on the beat and the other between beats, so slower songs work too. [170 BPM running songs](/rundown/170-bpm-running-songs) explains where that popular number comes from.

## To raise cadence, add songs 3 to 5% faster

- Pick songs 3 to 5% above your count. At 160, that's 165 to 168 BPM, such as the [165 BPM list](/music/bpm/165).
- Run to them for the last few minutes of an easy run, not the whole run.
- Do it once or twice a week. Your step rate drifts up without thinking about it.
- If the beat feels like a leash, go back to your own rhythm.

Your stride gets a little shorter as cadence rises. That's the trade, and it's the reason to keep changes small. Pain is a question for a doctor or physical therapist, not a cadence app.
