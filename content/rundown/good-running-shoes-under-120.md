**Short answer:** The Nike Winflo 12 ($110) is the default under $120. Cheaper shoes give up the newest bouncy foams, not fit, durability or weight.

## Under $120 you lose bounce and little else

The ten most popular daily trainers in our data average $151.50. One shelf down, most of the difference is foam. Budget midsoles are firmer EVA blends: durable and predictable, without the spring of a $170 trainer. You notice it most late in long runs.

Weight is close. The $110 [Winflo 12](/shoes/nike-winflo-12) is 9.7 oz, and the $145 Pegasus 42 is 9.8 oz. Fit and durability don't drop with price either, and no study has shown that pricier shoes lower injury risk.

## Five worth buying

- Nike Winflo 12, $110, 9.7 oz, 10 mm drop. Moderate cushion and a durable outsole. The pick if you're not sure what you want.
- [ASICS GT-1000 15](/shoes/asics-gt-1000-15), $100, 9.4 oz. Moderate support for $100, tied with its older version as the cheapest in our data. The pick if you want a stability shoe.
- [Brooks Launch 12](/shoes/brooks-launch-12), $115, 8.5 oz. Firm and quick, light enough for strides and tempo runs. Firm for long runs.
- [Brooks Revel 9](/shoes/brooks-revel-9), $100, 8.7 oz. A flexible, light shoe that doubles as an everyday sneaker. Firm over long distances.
- [New Balance Fresh Foam 680 v9](/shoes/new-balance-fresh-foam-680-v9), $85, 9.1 oz. Soft, comes in several widths, and the best value in the group.

The Nike Revolution 8 at $70 sits one step below: firm, basic cushioning and not built for long runs. Prices are list prices from our data, and all five are often on sale for less. The full list is on [running shoes under $120](/shoes/best/running-shoes-under-120).

## When paying more makes sense

- You run more than about 35 miles a week. Softer foam helps on tired legs.
- You've found a fit nothing cheaper matches.
- You're adding a second, faster shoe. A [two-pair rotation](/rundown/shoe-rotation-two-pairs) built on a $110 trainer and a $150 one beats one $260 shoe doing every job.
