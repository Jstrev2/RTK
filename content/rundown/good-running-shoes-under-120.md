The average price of the ten most popular daily trainers in our database is about $150. None of them will make you meaningfully healthier than the shoes one shelf down. The budget tier is where brands quietly sell last year's ideas at honest prices, and for most training miles that's exactly what you want.

Here's the deal you're actually making, followed by the shoes worth your money.

## What you give up under $120

Mostly, you give up the newest foams. Budget midsoles are typically firmer EVA blends — durable and predictable, but without the bounce that makes a $170 trainer feel like it's helping. You'll notice it most late in long runs, when softer, lighter foam stops feeling like a luxury.

What you don't reliably give up is weight — the $110 [Winflo 12](/shoes/nike-winflo-12) is 9.7 oz, and the $145 Pegasus 42 is 9.8. What you do give up is race-day snap; there are no plates down here, and there shouldn't be.

## What you don't give up

Fit, durability, and injury protection. Budget uppers are sometimes better than premium ones — simpler mesh, fewer clever ideas to rub you wrong. Firmer foams often outlast soft superfoams by hundreds of miles. And no study has ever shown expensive shoes reduce injury risk; comfort and consistency matter, price doesn't.

## The five worth buying

- **Default pick: [Nike Winflo 12](/shoes/nike-winflo-12), $110.** The boring, correct answer. Moderate cushion, 10mm drop, wide availability, and nothing about it needs an apology. If you're not sure what you want, this.
- **If you overpronate: [ASICS GT-1000 15](/shoes/asics-gt-1000-15), $100.** Real structured support at a hundred dollars — the cheapest legitimate stability trainer we track, and the pick if your old shoes wear hard on the inner edge.
- **If you want fast-day energy: [Saucony Kinvara 15](/shoes/saucony-kinvara-15), $120.** At 7.2 oz it's lighter than most $180 shoes. The catch is a firm ride and a 4mm drop that loads your calves — ease in if you're coming from 10mm.
- **If it's between shoes and groceries: [New Balance Fresh Foam 680 v9](/shoes/new-balance-fresh-foam-680-v9), $85.** A genuinely decent trainer, not a treadmill-and-errands shoe. The [Nike Revolution 8](/shoes/nike-revolution-8) at $70 exists one rung below it, and that rung is noticeable.
- **If you like a firmer, quicker feel every day: [Brooks Launch 12](/shoes/brooks-launch-12), $115.** Less pillow, more pop, 8.5 oz. The pick for runners who find the Winflo dull.

Prices are current list from our database; all five are frequently on sale below these numbers, at which point this whole tier becomes absurd value.

## When paying more is right

Spend up when you're past roughly 35 miles a week (softer foam earns its keep on tired legs), when you've found a specific fit nothing cheap matches, or when you're buying a second, faster shoe to go with a budget daily — a [rotation of two](/rundown/shoe-rotation-two-pairs) built around one $110 shoe and one $150 shoe beats a single $260 shoe doing everything badly.
