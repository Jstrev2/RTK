**Short answer:** Eat a normal-sized dinner of familiar, low-fiber carbs. The carb load is the 2 days before, not one pasta dinner.

## Carb loading is a 2-day job

Jeukendrup's 2011 review in the Journal of Sports Sciences gives a range of 5 to 12 g of carbs per kilogram of body weight a day, depending on the runner and the training load. It cites a study where about 13 g per kilogram raised muscle glycogen over about 10 g without improving endurance. A 2022 Gatorade Sports Science Institute review gives 7 to 12 g per kilogram scaled to the race. We use 8 to 10 as the target for the 2 days before.

So the dinner is one meal in a run of meals. Spread the carbs across the day.

## The math for your weight

Multiply body weight in kilograms by the target. Pounds divided by 2.2 gives kilograms.

- 55 kg (121 lb): 440 g at 8 g/kg, 550 g at 10 g/kg.
- 70 kg (154 lb): 560 g at 8 g/kg, 700 g at 10 g/kg.
- 85 kg (187 lb): 680 g at 8 g/kg, 850 g at 10 g/kg.

Most runners fall short of these numbers without trying. Liquid carbs such as juice or sports drink count toward it, and a gel adds 25 g or more; see the [gel table](/fuel/best/high-carb-gels).

## What goes on the plate

Choose foods you've eaten before a long run. Low fiber and low fat keep the stomach quiet.

- White rice, pasta, potatoes, bread, bagels.
- A moderate portion of lean protein, such as chicken or eggs.
- Skip: large salads, beans, heavy cream sauces, spicy food, new restaurants.

If the dinner is out, call ahead and ask for plain pasta or rice with a simple sauce.

## Timing and the morning after

Eat dinner at the usual time, or 2 hours earlier than usual if the start is early. Sip fluids through the evening. Don't force a second dinner.

On race morning, eat a familiar breakfast 2 to 4 hours before the start. Our [marathon gel guide](/rundown/how-many-gels-for-a-marathon) covers fuel during the race, and the [marathon fuel plans](/fuel/plan/marathon/4-00) list gels by mile.

## Who this is wrong for

This plan fits healthy runners racing 3 or more hours. It is wrong for:

- Anyone racing a 10K or half, who needs a normal dinner and no load.
- Runners with a medical diet, who should check with a doctor or dietitian.
- Runners who haven't practiced it. Try the food pattern before a long run first, as the [gut training guide](/rundown/train-your-gut-race-fueling) lays out.
