**Short answer:** Take about 3 mg of caffeine per kg of body weight, around an hour before the start. A 70 kg runner needs 210 mg, which is two 100 mg gels or a strong coffee.

## 3 mg per kg is the dose to start with

A 2008 review by Burke in Applied Physiology, Nutrition, and Metabolism found benefits at about 3 mg per kg across endurance events. A 2018 meta-analysis in Sports Medicine (Southward and colleagues) reported a small effect on endurance performance at 3 to 6 mg per kg. The effect is small, and the studies mostly used lab trials, not marathons.

- 55 kg: 165 mg
- 65 kg: 195 mg
- 75 kg: 225 mg
- 85 kg: 255 mg

Multiply your weight in kg by 3. Weight in pounds divided by 2.2 gives kg.

## Take it about an hour before, then top up late

The usual plan is to take the dose about 60 minutes before the start, so it peaks near the gun. That timing is our planning default, not a result we can tie to one trial. For races over three hours, a second smaller dose in the last third of the race is common practice, not a tested protocol.

## Count the caffeine in your gels

A caffeinated gel can double as part of your fueling plan. A 70 kg runner who eats two Maurten CAF 100 gels during a marathon has taken 200 mg, close to the full dose, and counted 50 grams of carbs toward the race. The [marathon gel guide](/rundown/how-many-gels-for-a-marathon) covers the rest of the count.

If you drink coffee before the start, count it. Coffee varies a lot by cup, so a gel with a printed number is easier to plan around than a mug.

## Who should skip it

More is not better. The same research that supports 3 mg per kg does not show reliable gains from much higher doses, and higher doses raise the odds of jitters and stomach trouble.

- Skip it if caffeine makes you anxious or gives you stomach trouble on training runs.
- Skip it if you're sensitive and race in the evening, when it can cost sleep.
- Don't try it for the first time on race day. Use it on a long run first, as part of [practicing your race fueling](/rundown/train-your-gut-race-fueling).
