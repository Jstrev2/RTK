**Short answer:** Leave half to a full thumb's width between your longest toe and the end of the shoe. For most runners that is a half size up from their street shoe size.

## The rule is a thumb's width of space

ASICS says to leave half to a full thumb's width between your toes and the end of the shoe, and to go up half a size if you have less. Brooks and REI give the same thumb's width guide. These are brand and retailer guidelines, not trial results.

To check, stand with the shoe laced and press down on the front. Your longest toe is often the second toe, not the big one.

## Feet swell, so try shoes on late

Nike, ASICS and others advise trying shoes on after a run or in the evening, when feet are larger. A shoe that fits at 8 a.m. may be tight at mile 8. Longer runs call for more room than a 5K does.

Try shoes on with the socks you run in. A thick sock can change the size you need by half a size.

## What a good fit feels like

- A snug heel that does not lift as you walk.
- A midfoot that holds without pressure on the top of the foot.
- Room to spread your toes side to side.
- A thumb's width of space in front of the longest toe.

Width matters as much as length. A shoe that is the right length but too narrow needs a wide size, not a bigger number. Our [wide feet guide](/rundown/wide-feet-running-shoes) covers that case.

## Who should not size up

Runners who already have room in front of the toes, or whose heel slips, should stay at their size. A bigger shoe will not fix a heel that lifts. If you buy without trying on, our guide to [buying shoes online](/rundown/buy-running-shoes-online-without-a-fitting) covers the check on day one.
