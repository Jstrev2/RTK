**Short answer:** Most running shoes last 300 to 500 miles. Check them at 300 and replace them when the midsole creases deep, the outsole wears through to foam or easy runs start to feel harsh.

## 300 to 500 miles is a range, not a deadline

The range is wide because foam, rubber and runners differ. A heavier runner compresses the midsole more each stride. A runner who scuffs a heel on pavement wears the outsole faster than one who lands softly on trails.

Treat 300 miles as the first check and 500 as the point to replace without more debate. Lab tests show midsole foam loses cushioning with use, but no study gives a mileage that fits every runner.

## Four signs that beat the odometer

- The midsole is creased: press a thumb into the foam. Deep wrinkles across it and a dead feel mean the bounce is gone.
- The outsole is worn through to foam at the heel or under the ball of the foot. Grip and protection go with it.
- The upper has failed: a collapsed heel counter or a torn toe box lets your foot slide.
- Your legs feel more beaten up after easy runs, and the shoe is past 300 miles. A hard training week can do the same, so it's a hint, not proof.

A side-by-side test settles it. Run the same easy route in the old pair one day and a newer pair the next. If the old one feels flat, replace it.

Pain that keeps coming back is a question for a doctor or physical therapist, not a shoe swap.

## Soft shoes wear out sooner than firm ones

A firm shoe feels about the same at mile 1 and mile 300. A soft one changes more, because the foam is the ride. The [Nike Winflo 12](/shoes/nike-winflo-12) is firm with a durable outsole and ages well. The [Hoka Clifton 11](/shoes/hoka-clifton-11) is 42 mm of soft EVA, and you'll feel it go flat.

Daily trainers built for mileage, like the [Nike Pegasus 42](/shoes/nike-pegasus-42), list durability as a strength in our data.

## Two pairs last longer than one

Alternating pairs gives the foam a day to recover between runs. A 2015 study of 264 runners over 22 weeks found those who rotated more than one pair had a 39% lower injury risk (Malisoux and colleagues). It was observational, so it can't prove the rotation caused it. [Rotating two pairs](/rundown/shoe-rotation-two-pairs) shows how to set it up.
