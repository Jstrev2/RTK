**Short answer:** Peak at 25 to 35 miles a week to finish your first marathon, with a longest run of 18 to 20 miles. Aim for 35 to 50 if you have a time goal.

## Finishing needs 25 to 35 miles a week at peak

Hal Higdon's Novice 1 plan runs 18 weeks, four runs a week, averages 24 miles a week and tops out at a 20-mile long run. That is the common template for a first marathon, and it works because most of the load sits in one long run.

Four runs a week is enough. Five adds mileage without adding much you need on race day.

## A time goal needs 35 to 50 miles

Faster goals ask for more running at moderate effort, which means more weekly miles and a second harder day. These are planning ranges, not a research result:

- Finish, no time goal: 25 to 35 miles at peak, 4 runs a week.
- 4:30 to 4:00: 30 to 40 miles, 4 to 5 runs.
- 4:00 to 3:30: 35 to 45 miles, 5 runs.
- Under 3:30: 45 to 55 miles, 5 to 6 runs.

Pick the row from your goal on the [pace calculator](/tools/pace-calculator), not from a friend's mileage.

## Build by about 10% a week, with a lighter week every fourth

A 2014 Danish study followed 874 new runners with GPS watches and sorted them by how fast their weekly distance grew. Injury rates did not differ significantly between groups. Runners who added more than 30% over two weeks had a hazard ratio of 1.59 against those under 10% (confidence interval 0.96 to 2.66, P = .07).

That is one observational study of beginners. It supports a slow build, and nothing in it supports a jump. Cut mileage by about a quarter every fourth week.

## Start from the miles you already run

Begin training at the weekly mileage you can already run without stiffness the next morning. If that is 15 miles, a 25-mile peak is a 10-week build at 10% a week. If it is 5 miles, plan a base block before the 18-week plan starts.

The long run grows by 1 to 2 miles at a time and stays at 20 or under. Longer than 20 adds recovery cost and little else for most runners.

## Shoes and fuel scale with the miles

More miles means more shoe wear. A [two-shoe rotation](/rundown/shoe-rotation-two-pairs) spreads it, and the [shoe selector](/tools/shoe-selector) narrows the choice. Long runs past 90 minutes also need fuel: the [marathon gel math](/rundown/how-many-gels-for-a-marathon) gives the count by finish time.

This guide is for healthy runners. If a build leaves you in pain, see a doctor or physical therapist before adding miles.
