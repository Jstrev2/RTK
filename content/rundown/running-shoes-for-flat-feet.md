**Short answer:** Flat feet don't require a stability shoe. If you want support, the ASICS GT-1000 15 is $100 and 9.4 oz, and a neutral trainer is fine if it feels better.

## Flat feet didn't predict injuries in the main study

In 2014 Nielsen and colleagues followed 927 novice runners for a year in the same neutral shoe (British Journal of Sports Medicine). Runners with pronated feet did not have a significantly higher injury risk than runners with neutral feet after 250 km. It is one study of beginners in one shoe, so it says nothing about what a stability shoe does for a particular person.

Nigg and colleagues (2015, same journal) proposed that runners pick shoes through a "comfort filter", meaning the shoe that feels best is a reasonable guide. That is a proposal, not a settled result.

## The GT-1000 15 is the default

The [ASICS GT-1000 15](/shoes/asics-gt-1000-15) is $100, 9.4 oz, with a 35 mm stack and 8 mm drop. Our data calls it "strong value for supported miles" and lists a basic foam package as its limit. It is the cheapest current stability trainer in the data, so it costs little to find out whether you like the feel.

## Other supported options from our data

- [Saucony Omni 22](/shoes/saucony-omni-22): $140, 10.1 oz. Our notes call it a pick for "overpronators and flat-footed runners who want structure", with a dull ride and a long break-in.
- [Brooks Adrenaline GTS 26](/shoes/brooks-adrenaline-gts-26): $155, 10.8 oz, 10 mm drop. Wide sizing options.
- [Saucony Guide 19](/shoes/saucony-guide-19): $145, 9.4 oz, 6 mm drop. Stable and planted, uninspiring for speed work.
- [Saucony Echelon 11](/shoes/saucony-echelon-11): $155, 11.5 oz. Roomy, and our data says it fits custom orthotics.

## Test it before you commit

Jog a few minutes in a supportive shoe and a neutral one with similar cushion. Keep the one you notice least. The [stability versus neutral guide](/rundown/stability-shoes-vs-neutral-do-you-need-support) covers the test in more detail.

If you switch types, run one or two easy runs in the new pair before a long run. The [shoe selector](/tools/shoe-selector) can filter by stability.

## Who this is wrong for

- Runners who use prescribed orthotics. Check the fit with them in the shoe, and start with the Echelon 11.
- Heavier runners who want a wider base. The [heavier runners guide](/rundown/running-shoes-for-heavier-runners) fits better.
- Anyone with foot pain. See a doctor or physical therapist, because a shoe won't treat it.
